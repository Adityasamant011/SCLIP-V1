## A. Scientific research and professional standards

### 1. A Window on Reality: Perceiving Edited Moving Images
- Authors: Tim J. Smith, Daniel Levin, James E. Cutting (2012)
- URL: https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21182/2/cf891501-bc1c-4904-bcee-5170766e6e53.pdf
- Read in full. Supports: attentional synchrony (editing steers viewers' eyes to the same place); match-on-action cut masking (~1/3 of cuts during coherent motion missed vs ~1/10 between static scenes); blink entrainment at cuts; event segmentation in film comprehension; change blindness (viewers build no detailed allocentric scene map, giving editors continuity latitude).
- Limitations: lab stimuli, mostly narrative film clips; effect sizes for online short-form viewing are assumed, not measured.

### 2. Attention and the Evolution of Hollywood Film
- Authors: James E. Cutting, Jordan E. DeLong, Christine E. Nothelfer (2010)
- URL: https://jordandelong.com/pubs/2010/AttentionEvolution.pdf
- Read in full. Supports: shot lengths across 150 films cluster in "packets" with a 1/f pattern; this temporal structure correlates with attention engagement. Basis for "pacing in waves, not metronomes."
- Limitations: correlational; Hollywood features, not social video. We use it as evidence against uniform pacing, not as a target distribution.

### 3. EEG/ERP study on shot scale changes and 180-degree violations
- Frontiers in Neuroscience (2023)
- URL: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2023.1173704/full
- Read in full. Supports: scale-in cuts (wide-to-close) processed more fluently than scale-out; 180-degree axis violations produce an orienting cost.
- Limitations: EEG proxies, controlled stimuli; we cite directionally.

### 4. Research-Based Principles for Designing Multimedia Instruction (Mayer)
- Author: Richard E. Mayer (Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning chapter, UNH-hosted PDF)
- URL: https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf
- Read in full. Supports (with experiment counts and median effect sizes): coherence (remove extraneous material; 18/19 tests, ES 0.86), signaling (highlight essential material; 26/28, ES 0.70), redundancy (don't duplicate narration as full on-screen text; 8/12, ES 0.10 - with boundary conditions: short or reworded text can be fine), spatial contiguity (words near graphics; 9/9, ES 0.82), temporal contiguity (graphics simultaneous with narration; 8/8, ES 1.31), segmenting (learner-paced chunks; 7/7, ES 0.67), modality (spoken over printed words with graphics; 18/19, ES 1.00, with boundary conditions).
- Limitations: learning-transfer studies, mostly instructional animations; we apply to educational/tutorial content design and general comprehension logic, and we preserved the boundary conditions in the KB entries.

### 5. BBC Subtitle Guidelines
- BBC (accessibility guidelines, current version) and archived editorial guidelines
- URLs: https://www.bbc.co.uk/accessibility/forproducts/guides/subtitles/ ; https://web.archive.org/web/20191020222240/http:/bbc.github.io/subtitle-guidelines/
- Read in full. Supports: 160-180 wpm recommended subtitle speed (~0.3s/word minimum display); max two lines; line breaks at linguistically coherent points; positioning to avoid covering critical picture; editing/timing prioritized over line-break aesthetics; online width constraints (e.g., 90% width for 9:16 vertical).
- Limitations: broadcast accessibility standard; short-form word-by-word caption styles are a different (style) convention, flagged as such in the KB.

### 6. Influence of Music on Perceived Emotions in Film (review + experiment)
- HAL open archive preprint
- URL: https://hal.science/hal-04114230v2/file/Influence_of_Music_on_Perceived_Emotions_in_Film_AAM-3.pdf
- Read in full. Supports: film music significantly affects perceived emotions; the same scene with different soundtracks yields different plot interpretations and anticipated outcomes (Bullerjahn & Guldenring and related studies); viewers interpret characters' emotions through the music (Tan et al.); music loudness changes can shift interpretation more than changing the piece (Tan, Spackman & Wakefield 2017); matching-score accuracy drops without humans in the scene.
- Limitations: small-sample studies, film-clip stimuli; we cite as directional evidence that music is interpretation, not decoration.

### 7. Soviet montage theory (reference) and Eisenstein's methods of montage
- URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory ; https://film110.pbworks.com/f/Eisenstein+Methods+of+Montage.pdf ; https://www.monoskop.org/images/7/7c/Eisenstein_Sergei_Film_Form_Essays_in_Film_Theory_1977.pdf
- Consulted. Supports: the five montage methods (metric, rhythmic, tonal, overtonal, intellectual) used as descriptive levers in visual-editing.md and content-types.md. Film theory, treated as framework, not empirical finding.

## B. Expert creators and editors

### 8. Kallaway - short-form craft (three sources)
- "How To Hook People In The First 3 Seconds" and related videos (transcripts): https://pickscribe.com/v/96HQjrqA_s4 and https://pickscribe.com/v/9K1b6dSdc50
- "The Psychology of Killer Hooks" (moderncreator.app, 2026-08-05): https://moderncreator.app/2026-08-05-kallaway-the-psychology-of-killer-hooks
- Read in full. Supports: comprehension maxing; visual matching; hawkeye (broad-to-deep) structure; proof-first; hooks as engineered contrast; the lock-in zone (post-hook confirmation/trust window); value compression; rehooks and open loops in long-form; six-level creator skill model.
- Limitations: one creator's system, validated by his own results and teaching, not independent study. Used as expert practice, cross-checked against Film Booth and Galloway where they overlap.

### 9. Hayden Hillier-Smith (two interviews)
- Forbes (2023-03-10) and Musicbed interview: https://www.musicbed.com/articles/filmmaking/masterminding-viral-edits-with-hayden-hillier-smith/
- Read in full. Supports: emotion/story/rhythm in every frame; leitmotifs as structural landmarks; slow cuts build tension, fast cuts pay off; storyshowing over storytelling (Stanton's 2+2); music-first workflow (choose music early, let it shape the emotional map; "the difference between comedy and horror is the music"); proactive editing; less-is-more timed deliberately (Civil War example); context defines content (an uncommitted YouTube viewer needs different pacing than a cinema audience).
- Limitations: expert opinion; the music-first workflow conflicts with Steed Films' music-near-end advice - the disagreement is documented in audio-editing.md rather than resolved.

### 10. Film Booth (Ed Lawrence) - Creators on Air interview

### 13. Documentary editing - ScriptCut workflow article and IDA's Zen of Editing review

## D. Known gaps and caveats

- **Premiere Gal** (named in the brief): her Adobe profile (source 58) covers identity and output, not editorial reasoning, and her tutorial catalog is tool-mechanics rather than why-focused. A dedicated technique-reasoning source for her does not appear to exist; flagged, not fabricated.
- **Speech rhythm and prosody research** (brief topic): partially covered - Guo (53) and Yokoyama & Daibo (57) address speech rate and perception; the dialogue-rhythm guidance in audio-editing.md rests on practitioner sources (see sections 13 and 16). Still missing: a lab study tying exact cut placement to phrase/stress boundaries in speech.
- **Video summarization / computational editing papers**: reviewed during recon (AutoCut CVPR 2026, Google AutoFlip, event-segmentation-adjacent work); they informed system context more than editorial principles, so they are not cited in the KB docs.
- **Creator claims are not laws.** Kallaway, Hillier-Smith, Galloway, and Film Booth sometimes disagree with each other and with film-craft tradition; where they do (music-first vs music-near-end; beat-sync debates), the KB documents both positions and the conditions for each rather than picking a winner.
- **Source count:** 70 external sources (49 V1 + 21 deepening pass), 100% primary or clearly-labeled analyst teardowns.

- **No invented numbers.** Every numeric value in the KB (subtitle wpm, frames-per-beat math, effect sizes, select-count workflow figures) traces to a source above and is labeled with its scope. Where ranges are working conventions (B-roll 2-5s), they are tagged as guidance, not rules.
